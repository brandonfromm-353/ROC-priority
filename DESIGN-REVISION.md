# Simplification review and meaningful revision

## Review of the first output

The first version communicated the intended flow, but its rank bar, percentile, point total, separate target-game card, slogan, and progress strip added competing signals. The rank bar did not explain a concrete entry-group threshold. Screen 3 offered a link back to opportunities, while its link home depended on recognizing the brand as navigation.

## Revision implemented

| Criterion | Change | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability and fundamental value at first glance | The landing heading now reads “Know your priority. Decide what’s worth your time.” One sentence connects another event to earlier entry. | Makes the capability and the value of control explicit before the student reads the data. |
| Supporting content serves the primary job | Removed the rank bar, percentile, extra slogan, and multi-step navigation strip. Points retain the original separated row with a bold value. There is one primary action. | Reduces competing signals and prioritizes rank, entry group, and the next decision. |
| Obvious navigation home | Every screen has a labeled “My ROC Standing” link in the same position. Screen 3 also has “Compare Another Event.” | Students can return home directly without interpreting the logo or backing through the flow. |
| Gestalt: common region and proximity | Combined current rank and target-game entry into one card. Paired time and estimated points in each event card. Restored separate before/after cards with matching labels and a neutral target-game takeaway, grouped by proximity. | Related facts are understood as parts of the same decision. |
| Gestalt: similarity and continuity | Reused the same card treatment, typography, navy buttons, navigation position, and spacing across all three screens. Current and projected results use aligned labels and values. | Students recognize a consistent product and can scan the comparison with less effort. |
| Meaningful revision | Reorganized the information hierarchy and grouping, removed ambiguous visual information, and added explicit navigation. | This changes communication and comprehension, rather than merely changing colors. |

## Scope and validation

Exactly three primary screens remain. Each event leads to its own mock projection; the opportunities screen does not reveal the complete result. All screens retain the shared mock-data disclaimer.

Programmatic checks passed for the landing content, explicit home links across all screens, three event-specific outcomes, comparison return links, and deferred before/after information. These checks do not replace student usability evaluation.

Suggested evaluation prompts: “Where do you stand for BYU vs. Notre Dame?”, “Which event fits your time?”, “Would it change your entry group?”, and “Go back to your standing.” Observe whether students can answer without explanation.

## Preference refinement

Restored the original priority-points row and the original impact-page presentation: event summary, separate current/projected cards with point totals, directional arrow, and neutral outcome panel. The explicit home links and simplified landing message remain.
